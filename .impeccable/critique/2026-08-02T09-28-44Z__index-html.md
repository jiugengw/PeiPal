## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** The flow feels authored for Count Me In rather than interchangeable with a generic account wizard. The trusted-circle preview, approval boundary, mobility and transport fields, and low-pressure copy all express the product's distinctive mechanism. The main missed opportunity is making the older adult's agency visible in the setup itself: the interface currently reads primarily as a family member configuring care support for someone else.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs --json index.html` returned zero findings. No automated visual anti-patterns were reported. There is no reliable browser overlay for this run because no browser connection was available.

## Overall Impression

This is a thoughtful, unusually humane setup flow with a clear visual voice. The biggest opportunity is trust clarity: make it unmistakable what information is being collected, what the older adult controls, what the family member controls, and what will happen after confirmation.

## What's Working

- The opening screen establishes the relationship model immediately: “someone they trust,” “trusted circle,” and “main approver” are stronger than generic onboarding language.
- The six-step structure gives a complex setup task a manageable shape, and the mobile collapse is sensible.
- The green/cream/coral palette, circular avatars, and orbit motif create a recognizable support-circle identity without needing photography or fabricated testimonials.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The older adult's agency is underrepresented

**Why it matters:** The setup repeatedly frames the family member as the operator: “Set up care support for your loved one,” “Only you can approve,” and “Tell us about your loved one.” That may accidentally position the older adult as a passive subject rather than a participant.

**Fix:** Add an explicit agency statement near the start, such as “You’re helping set this up together. Mary stays in control of what she wants to do and what gets shared.” Distinguish “setup permissions” from “the older adult’s choices,” and include the older adult in the review language.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Approval and sharing boundaries arrive too late

**Why it matters:** The most sensitive product promise—who can approve, what trusted contacts can do, and what is not sent—appears across separate screens. Users must assemble the policy mentally before reaching review.

**Fix:** Add a compact persistent “How support works” summary to the first and review screens: “Mary chooses the activity. Jamie approves sharing. Contacts can offer help, but cannot approve for her.” Use explicit labels for “choose,” “approve,” and “support.”

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The demo defaults blur the boundary between example data and real data

**Why it matters:** Pre-filled “Mary Lim,” “Toa Payoh,” and named contacts make the prototype easy to demo, but a real family member could mistake them for saved information or accidentally confirm the wrong profile.

**Fix:** Either label the state clearly as “Example setup,” or start blank in product mode and keep demo data behind a “Load example” affordance. Before confirmation, add a visible “This is example information” treatment if demo data remains.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P2] Progress is visually clear but not fully accessible or actionable

**Why it matters:** The active and completed states are communicated through color and styling, while the `ol` items do not expose current-step semantics. A keyboard or screen-reader user may not know where they are in the setup.

**Fix:** Add `aria-current="step"` to the active item, meaningful list semantics, and a text status such as “Step 2 of 6.” Ensure the same status is available on mobile when the sidebar becomes a compact grid.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

### [P2] The flow has limited recovery and persistence

**Why it matters:** A family member may need to pause, check a detail, or return later. The current flow offers Back but no save state, draft language, or clear recovery if the page reloads.

**Fix:** Add “Saved locally” or “Not saved yet” status, preserve entered fields during the session, and make the review screen support direct editing of each section. If persistence is intentionally out of scope, say so clearly.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

## Persona Red Flags

**Older adult participating with family:** The flow speaks about the person more often than it speaks with them. “Your loved one” and “Only you can approve” can feel disempowering unless paired with a clear shared-control statement. The preferred language and mobility fields are a strong inclusion signal, but the reason each is needed is not explained.

**Family-member first-timer:** The flow is approachable, but the distinction between contact support roles and approval authority is distributed across screens. They may add someone as a support contact without understanding that “help with booking” still does not authorize a final decision.

**Keyboard or screen-reader user:** Visual progress states are likely understandable by sight but are not fully represented in semantics. The add/remove contact controls have titles, but the dynamic contact rows do not visibly announce when a row is added, removed, or invalid.

## Minor Observations

- The copy is warm and specific, but some screens repeat “important plans” and “trusted contacts” without a short consistent definition.
- The completion state is reassuring; it could be stronger if it named the next user action as an actual button choice rather than only explanatory text.
- The visual system is distinctive enough for the prototype, but the current palette and 8px geometry are still provisional according to `DESIGN.md`.
- The detector found no issues, but automated cleanliness should not be mistaken for accessibility validation.

## Questions to Consider

- Should the older adult be invited to participate in setup directly, or is this intentionally a family-only setup flow?
- What is the single trust promise users must remember at confirmation: who chooses, who approves, or what is never sent without consent?
- Do you want demo mode and product mode to be visibly different, or should the prototype behave like a blank real setup from the first screen?
